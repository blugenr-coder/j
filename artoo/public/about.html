What "training" means here

The 3D shape itself comes from an image-to-3D engine — a neural network trained by its maker on millions of 3D objects. Artoo does not retrain that network: doing so takes rented data-centre GPUs and a large 3D dataset, which is a separate project.

What Artoo trains is your model's choice of settings: engine version, polygon budget, quads or triangles, textures and so on. Each rating updates its estimate of how good each setting variant is (a method called Thompson sampling). It keeps trying untested variants now and then, and uses the winners more and more.

Every rated pair — image in, 3D model out, your score — is also kept as a dataset you can download from the Models page. That is the raw material for a real fine-tune of an open-source 3D network later, if you decide it is worth it.

Engines

  • Meshy — real image-to-3D through the Meshy API: full shape including the back, textures, GLB download. Needs an API key on the server and uses Meshy credits for every generation.
  • Browser — free, in your browser, about a second. It cuts out the subject, gives every part a round, width-aware thickness, smooths the surface and paints the photo back on. Hidden sides are a guess, so use Meshy when the back matters.
